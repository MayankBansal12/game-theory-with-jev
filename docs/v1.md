# v1: opening scenarios and strategy comparisons

The UI contains two versions: **v0** and **v1**. v0 preserves the original eight-opponent pilot. v1 is one experiment with three scenarios, 20 matches per opponent per scenario (4× v0), and 20 rounds per match.

| Scenario | Jev’s first move | Opponents | Matches | Rounds | Model decisions |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Free opening | Jev chooses | 12 | 240 | 4,800 | 5,200 |
| Cooperate first | Forced C | 12 | 240 | 4,800 | 4,960 |
| Defect first | Forced D | 12 | 240 | 4,800 | 4,960 |
| Total | | | 720 | 14,400 | 15,120 |

Only the primary Jev’s first move changes between scenarios. From round 2 it receives the actual history and scores and chooses freely. Forced moves have no model call, probability, or confidence. The second Jev in self-play always chooses freely.

The model, prompt, payoffs, known match length, and roster are identical across scenarios. Scripted randomness uses the same opponent/repetition seed across scenarios; this does not control Jev’s API sampling. Rounds within a match are dependent. Opponent extensions were recorded later, so service drift is a possible confound.

## Added opponents

- **Delayed Betrayal:** cooperate in rounds 1–5, defect once in round 6, then cooperate again. Jev first sees the betrayal in round 7 and renewed cooperation in round 8.
- **Bully:** start D, then play the opposite of Jev’s previous move.
- **Anti–Tit for Tat:** start C, then play the opposite of Jev’s previous move.
- **Adaptive:** cooperate in rounds 1–6, defect in rounds 7–11, then choose the action that has earned more accumulated points. Ties favor defection. Scores are recalculated from completed history, without hidden future information.

Bully and Anti–Tit for Tat follow the [Axelrod definitions](https://github.com/Axelrod-Python/Axelrod/blob/dev/axelrod/strategies/titfortat.py). Adaptive follows the [actual Axelrod implementation](https://github.com/Axelrod-Python/Axelrod/blob/dev/axelrod/strategies/adaptive.py), which compares total points despite its inline comment referring to averages. With these nonnegative payoffs, the selected action’s cumulative advantage cannot decrease while it keeps choosing that action.

Adaptive replaces Joss in all three public scenarios. Joss’s 60 recordings are retained in unpublished local archive runs, with their attempts transferred to those archives. Adaptive uses 60 newly recorded matches. Other matches retain their IDs, moves, and model responses. The static manifest removes retired Joss replays from publication.
